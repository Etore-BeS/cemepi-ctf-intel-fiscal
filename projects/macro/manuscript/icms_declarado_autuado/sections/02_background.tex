\section{Institutional Background}\label{sec:background}

ICMS (\emph{Imposto sobre Circula\c{c}\~ao de Mercadorias e Servi\c{c}os}) is levied by each Brazilian state on the circulation of goods and on electricity, interstate and intermunicipal transport, and communication.
Tax paid on inputs generates a credit against tax due on later transactions.
The OECD classification places general taxes on value added under heading 5111, whatever the stage at which they are charged \citep{oecd2026guide}.
An OECD working paper describes ICMS as a state-level VAT \citep{arnold2025}, and published studies treat it as Brazil's state value-added tax \citep{gomes2024,cabello2020}.
We use \vat{} for that object.
Labels that come from the administrative file keep their Portuguese names, because those names are the values in the data.

The European VAT is the usual international benchmark for this kind of tax.
It is an invoice-credit tax, and the chain of invoices is a source of third-party information \citep{keen2006}.
ICMS shares that logic and is classified with VAT.
It is not an EU tax, and the states set important parts of the base and the rates.
Those differences are context.
They are not the result we report.
The evidence below is the enrolled stock in one state.

The path from the tax to the enrolled certificate has two doors and then a common collection stage.
In the ordinary door, the firm reports the tax and does not pay it.
In the assessment door, the administration fixes the tax in an infringement notice after a review of the firm's records.
If the amount is not settled in the administrative phase, the state enrols it in the \emph{d\'ivida ativa}.
Each enrolled debt receives a certificate, the \emph{certid\~ao de d\'ivida ativa} (CDA).
The state attorney office, PGE-SP, collects the certificate, in court or out of it.
Payment is recorded on the state payment slip (GARE) linked to the certificate.
The state can also allow an enrolled debt to be paid in instalments.
We do not study those arrangements.
The file we use records payments on the certificate, not the terms of a programme.

In the source file, and in the classification used by the attorney office, the two doors have stable names.
Declared debt is the type \texttt{ICMS Declarado}: tax the taxpayer reported and did not pay.
Assessed debt is \texttt{ICMS Autua\c{c}\~ao} together with \texttt{ICMS Autua\c{c}\~ao Simples Nacional}: tax established by an assessment, including assessments of firms in the simplified federal regime.
Two further ICMS types, Fecoep and a GIA amendment, are enrolled as well.
They are small, and we leave them out of the segment comparisons.
The comparison is between declared debt and assessed debt.
