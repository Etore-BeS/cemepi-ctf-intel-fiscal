\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

All series come from one saved extract, \texttt{extracao=2026-03}.
The extract is not updated during the study.
If a source file is missing, we skip that grain.
We do not fill in a series that was not in the extract.

\paragraph{Monthly panel.}
The monthly file pairs enrolled-debt stock with collection.
It has $n = 119$ months and 14 columns.
The target is total collection, \texttt{valor\_total}.
The stock field used as a covariate is \texttt{valor\_sem\_honorarios}.
Tabular models on this grain use lags 1, 3, and 12 of collection and the same lags of stock.
The test block is 12 months.
That is a short sample for a large model.

\paragraph{Grain A: calendar-daily GARE.}
The daily file is an aggregate of GARE payment slips.
Each civil day has the sum of slip values, \texttt{valor\_sum}, and the number of slips, \texttt{n\_guias}.
We place the file on a continuous daily calendar.
Missing days become zeros in both fields.
The target is \texttt{valor\_sum}.
Zeros stay in the target.
They are weekends and days with no slip.
Ranking A is test sMAPE on every calendar day, zeros included.
A side score, sMAPE on days with $y \gt 0$, describes days that had a slip.
That side score does not replace Ranking A.

\paragraph{Grain B: business days.}
Grain B keeps Monday--Friday rows of the same slip aggregate.
This extract has $n = 2672$ such days.
Brazilian national holidays stay in the series.
No model receives a holiday indicator.
Grain B has its own train, validation, and test blocks.
It is not the grain A test block with weekends removed after the fact.

\paragraph{Weekly grain.}
We sum daily \texttt{valor\_sum} and \texttt{n\_guias} by week ending Sunday.
The seasonal naive on this grain copies the value 52 weeks earlier.
We do not use weeks that start on Monday.

\paragraph{Which models see which columns.}
Ordinary least squares, ridge, gradient boosting, the random forest, the Gaussian process, and kernel ridge use the tabular columns below.
The seasonal naive, SARIMAX, Prophet, the perceptron, TimesFM, and the two samplers E3 and E4 see the history of the target.
Prophet also uses the date index to build its seasonal terms.
Calendar-daily tabular columns are lags 1, 7, 14, and 28 of \texttt{valor\_sum} and of \texttt{n\_guias}, plus day of week and month.
Business-day tabular columns are lags 1, 5, 10, and 20, counted in business days, of the same two series, plus day of week and month.
Weekly tabular columns are lags 1, 4, 13, and 52 of the weekly sum and of the weekly slip count, plus week of year and month.
