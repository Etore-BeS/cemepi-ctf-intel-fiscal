\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

One protocol, applied to monthly, calendar-daily, business-daily, and weekly collection series, does not yield one best forecaster.
The March~2026 extract ranks families differently by grain, and collapsing those ranks would misstate the evidence.

On the monthly panel the disagreement proxy E2 leads on test sMAPE ($\approx 10.9$).
Histogram gradient boosting and the lag-12 seasonal naive remain close behind it.
Selective TEM does not lead that grain.
E2 is not TEM: the proxy switches on absolute disagreement, and TEM switches on an energy threshold.

On calendar-daily slips, TimesFM leads (sMAPE $\approx 59.8$).
Most other families have negative skill against the lag-7 seasonal naive, and weekend and empty-slip zeros dominate the official score.
The positive-day sMAPE is a description of active days.
It is not Ranking~A.

On business days, TimesFM, histogram gradient boosting, and ordinary least squares form the leading cluster.
Many positive skill scores on this grain reflect a weak lag-5 reference.
National holidays were not removed and were not supplied as regressors.

On the weekly aggregate, TimesFM shows the largest skill against the lag-52 seasonal naive (sMAPE $\approx 17.2$, skill $\approx 0.41$), with ridge and ordinary least squares next.

For monitoring, the seasonal naive remains the reference forecast on every grain.
Selective TEM is the specified instrument when a forecast may be withheld, and that use requires coverage beside sMAPE.
The proxy stays an ablation.
TimesFM is the zero-shot lead on the fine grains in this freeze, and it is not the monthly lead.

Subsequent work on the same extract should publish E1 coverage with the point scores, give Ranking~B an explicit holiday calendar, and keep the seasonal naive of each grain as the skill reference so that later freezes remain comparable.
