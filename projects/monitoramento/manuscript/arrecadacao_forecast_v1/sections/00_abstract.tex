Forecasting how much enrolled tax debt will be collected is a monitoring problem with a sticky stock and a seasonal, often sparse, cash series.
We compare fourteen forecasters under one protocol on four grains from a static administrative extract: a monthly panel of debt stock and collection, a calendar-daily aggregate of payment slips that keeps structural zeros, a Monday--Friday ablation of that daily series, and a weekly sum.
Stage~1 covers seasonal naive forecasts, linear and kernel regression, SARIMAX, histogram gradient boosting, Prophet, a multilayer perceptron, an adaptive random forest, a Gaussian process, and zero-shot TimesFM~3.0.
Stage~2 covers selective forecasting with the Time-Energy Model (TEM), a disagreement-proxy ablation that is not TEM, and reduced diffusion and score-based samplers.
Hyperparameters minimize validation symmetric MAPE (sMAPE).
The official rank is raw test sMAPE.
A complementary skill score uses Murphy's form against the seasonal naive of the same grain.
On the March~2026 extract the leader depends on the grain.
The disagreement proxy leads the monthly panel (sMAPE $\approx 10.9$).
TimesFM~3.0 leads the calendar-daily, business-daily, and weekly series.
Selective TEM remains a candidate monitoring rule when abstention is reported with the point forecast.
It is not the monthly sMAPE leader in this freeze, and the proxy ablation does not inherit the TEM label.
