\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

VAT (ICMS) is a state value-added tax.
This paper forecasts VAT collection, not the mix of every enrolled tax.
The legal-entity book has more than one ICMS type.
Declarado is tax the debtor reported.
Autuado is tax from an assessment (\texttt{ICMS Autua\c{c}\~ao}).
Together those two types are almost the whole book.
They are not the same series.
Autuado is most of the stock.
Declarado is most of the cash collected.
Section~\ref{sec:data} reports the shares.
A model that fits the total can hide a miss on one of the two types.

The cash series is seasonal.
At a daily step it is also full of zeros.
A zero is a weekend, a holiday, or a day with no payment slip.
Those zeros are part of grain A.
They are not missing values to drop before scoring.

A grain is the time step of a forecast.
This paper uses the four v1 grains: month, calendar day (grain A), Monday to Friday (grain B), and week.
Grain B keeps national holidays.
It is a different target.
It is not grain A with weekends removed after the score.

sMAPE is the symmetric mean absolute percentage error.
We define it in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}.
Lower sMAPE is better.
The official rank on every series and every grain is test sMAPE.
The skill score compares a model with the seasonal naive of that same grain.
We define it in Section~\ref{sec:ss}.
A positive skill score means lower sMAPE than that naive forecast.
A Diebold-Mariano test with the Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold correction asks whether that gap is larger than the noise in the test block~\citep{diebold1995comparing,harvey1997testing}.
A model confidence set lists the models the same block does not separate~\citep{hansen2011mcs}.
Neither test changes the rank.

TEM is the Time-Energy Model~\citep{brusokas2025tem}.
It scores candidate forecasts with an energy.
Abstention means refusing the energy choice and returning the seasonal naive instead.
We use abstention only for selective TEM, labeled E1.
E2 is a separate rule.
E2 switches between gradient boosting and the seasonal naive when those two forecasts disagree.
E2 does not estimate an energy.
E1 does not have a score in this run.
The vendor code is not in this worktree.

\paragraph{Questions.}
Which model has the lowest test sMAPE on each series and each grain?
Does that model change between the total, Declarado, and Autuado?
Does the test block separate that model from the seasonal naive, and from the other candidates?

\paragraph{What we report.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One protocol for three series and four grains.
        Models are chosen by validation sMAPE and ranked by test sMAPE.
        The skill score, the corrected Diebold-Mariano test, and the model confidence set are further columns.
        They are not the rank.
  \item Separate labels for E1 and E2.
        E1 abstains by an energy threshold.
        E2 abstains by absolute disagreement.
  \item The lowest test sMAPE belongs to different models on different series and on different grains.
        Keeping calendar zeros (A) or dropping weekends (B) changes both the sMAPE level and the order of models.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:results} gives the test sMAPE and reads each test.
