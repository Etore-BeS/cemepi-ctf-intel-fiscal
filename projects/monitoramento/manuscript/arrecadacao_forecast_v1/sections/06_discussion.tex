\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Ranks do not transfer across grains}
\label{sec:grain-dependence}

Table~\ref{tab:top3} does not support one best model.
The monthly panel is short, seasonal, and paired with a stock lag.
There, switching between boosting and the seasonal naive (E2) lowers test sMAPE relative to either forecast alone.

The daily and weekly GARE series are longer.
TimesFM has the lowest test sMAPE on those three grains.
TimesFM does not have the lowest test sMAPE on the monthly panel.
Table~\ref{tab:monthly} places it behind the seasonal naive, with a negative skill score.
The pretrained model wins on the long fine-grained series in this extract.
It does not win on the short monthly series.

E1 is the only row that estimates an energy and abstains by~\eqref{eq:tem}.
On the monthly test its sMAPE is higher than the seasonal naive.
The energy rule does not improve on that 12-month window.
The tables rank point sMAPE.
They do not report coverage, so they do not say how often E1 abstained.

Monthly and weekly sMAPE in Table~\ref{tab:top3} lie well below calendar-daily sMAPE.
A month or a week adds up many zero days, so the percentage error falls.
Skill scores tell a different comparison: a model can have a moderate sMAPE and still lose to the seasonal naive.
That is the common pattern on the monthly panel, on grain A, and on the weekly series outside TimesFM, ridge, and least squares.
Family-level skill bars are in Appendix~\ref{app:bars}.

\subsection{Zeros and the lag used in the skill score}
\label{sec:zeros}

On grain A, the lag-7 naive is second in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.
A copy of last week matches weekend zeros.
A model that smooths through those zeros pays for it in all-day sMAPE.
Scoring only days with $y \gt 0$ answers a different question: error on days that had a slip.
Ranking A is the error on the calendar, zeros included.

Grain B drops weekends and keeps national holidays (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
Test sMAPE falls, and boosting and least squares enter the top three behind TimesFM.
Skill on B uses lag 5.
Lag 1 already beats lag 5, so many skill scores are positive.
Those scores do not transfer to grain A, where the reference is lag 7.
Weekday holidays remain in the series.
No model, including Prophet, receives a holiday indicator.

\subsection{What the three leading rules actually return}
\label{sec:rules}

The seasonal naive is the reference forecast on every grain.
Its skill score is zero by construction.
On several grains it is inside the top three by test sMAPE.
A replacement has to beat it on the grain being published.

E1 returns the lowest-energy candidate, or the seasonal naive if that energy is at least $\tau$.
On the monthly extract this rule does not lower test sMAPE (about 15.5, against about 13.5 for the naive).
Until coverage is published next to sMAPE, the tables do not say how often E1 abstained.

E2 has the lowest monthly test sMAPE, about 10.9.
The rule is the validation-chosen switch in~\eqref{eq:proxy-rule}.
It shows that disagreement with the seasonal naive is a useful switch on this monthly window.
It does not estimate an energy.
A gain for E2 is not a gain for E1.

TimesFM has the lowest test sMAPE on grains A and B and on the weekly series.
Figures~\ref{fig:pred-m}, \ref{fig:pred-b}, and \ref{fig:pred-w} plot realized collection against the sMAPE leader on the monthly, business-day, and weekly grains (a second forecast is shown only when its test sMAPE is within 5\% of the leader).
The calendar-day (grain A) holdout is in Appendix~\ref{app:pred-a}.
Figure~\ref{fig:residual} shows the holdout residual $\hat y - y$ for those three leaders.
On the monthly figure the leader is E2, the disagreement proxy.
It is not E1.
On the business-day and weekly figures the leader is TimesFM.
If the TimesFM weights are unavailable, the seasonal naive is still defined.
On the monthly grain that naive remains competitive.
